Reflection

What I'd carry into the next system.

01

A generated score isn't a decision until a person can change it.

The draft rubric, the two scores and the reviewer's own call beside the AI's all come from the same belief: automation earns trust by being visible and editable, not by being invisible.

02

Shared structure with role-specific language beats bespoke screens.

One shell with different verbs scaled to every role and every tenant theme. Four separate products would have multiplied every fix by four.

03

What I'd validate next.

Whether recruiters keep opening the transcript and evidence behind a score once it has been right a few times, or start trusting the number outright. That shift changes how much detail the screen should keep surfacing.

My critique of the shipped design
  • Two visual weights for one idea. The job approval view is a light card; the timesheet view is denser. Level and approver rows should be identical.
  • Bypass is powerful and lightly guarded. It should require a reason and write it to history, the way closure does.
  • Financial detail is still long. A summary card with an expandable section per rate group would scan faster.
  • Terminology drifts. Approver, reviewer and recipient should be one word across the builder, the record and the dashboard.
What I'd do at 10× scale
  • Simulation. Let an admin test which approvers a sample transaction would reach before turning a workflow on.
  • Versioning. Edit a workflow without touching in-flight work, with publish and rollback.
  • Bottleneck analytics. Time per level and per approver, shown on the chain itself.
  • Smart queues. Sort Action required by age and value, not only by count.
  • Worker transparency. Show workers where their timesheet sits in the chain, not just the final status.
