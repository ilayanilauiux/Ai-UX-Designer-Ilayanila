02 — Operating views

From a single dashboard to role-specific operating views

Client, vendor, and MSP were opening one shell for three different accountabilities. I kept the shell and let the role decide which question it answers.

01 Context 02 Decision 03 Scope 04 Reflection

Context

Three jobs, one product

Simplify is a vendor management system for contingent labor. The client buys the labor. The vendor sends people. The MSP runs the program for the client. Those are the product’s own distinctions. I did not run a separate persona study for this page.

Decision

One shell. The question changes.

Designs 3.0 puts Client, Vendor, and MSP on the same structure: counts, a distribution, a calendar, and pending actions. A widget configuration lets someone pick and order what stays on the view, including Quick Links, Calendar, and Pending Actions. Choosing a role here rearranges that shell. It does not open a second product.

Scope

The questions, in the product’s words

  • ClientIs this program healthy? The buyer needs hiring, compliance, reporting, and invoicing under control.
  • VendorWhich jobs can I fill, and how do I stand? Supplier scorecards are how standing is shown.
  • MSPWhat needs a decision today, across this client’s suppliers? The MSP operates the system. The software is not the MSP.

The same board also holds a worker dashboard, a calendar widget, events, schedule selections, theme variations, and an earlier design pass. Those are context. They are not three more case studies. This page has no outcome percentages. The published interview figures belong to the hiring decision.

Reflection

What had to change

The shell can be shared. The question cannot.

Widget configuration is how a role keeps the pieces it is accountable for.

I designed three jobs in one product, not three products.
