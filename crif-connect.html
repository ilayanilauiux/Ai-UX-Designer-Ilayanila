CRIF Connect · Account Aggregator

Making data consent legible, reversible and auditable

CRIF Connect is an RBI-regulated Account Aggregator app. I designed the 0-to-1 mobile experience that turns a compliance framework into something people trust enough to use.

Role
Senior UI/UX Designer
Timeline
Dec 2021 — Oct 2024
Platform
iOS and Android, mobile-first

The context

Under India's Account Aggregator framework, people share financial data between institutions only with explicit consent they can give, pause or revoke at any time.

The framework is sound. The product problem is that "consent" is a legal object with a lifecycle, and most users have no mental model for it.

Constraints I designed within

  • Regulatory: consent must be explicit, specific, and revocable; every change needs an audit trail.
  • Security: sensitive actions need re-authentication (OTP and PIN).
  • Ecosystem: dozens of banks, insurers and fund houses, each with different account types and discovery behaviour.
  • Scope: a 0-to-1 mobile app for iOS and Android, designed inside DreamOrbit’s fintech practice.

Understanding the business

CRIF Connect is an RBI-licensed Account Aggregator. It moves a person's financial data from where it sits to where it is needed, only with their consent, and it never reads, stores or uses that data. That "data blind" promise is the product's core value, so the interface has to prove it.

What this meant for design: FIUs bring the consent requests, but the consumer owns the decision. The business wins only when users approve with confidence, so I treated every screen as either building or spending trust.

Three tensions, not three features

The brief said "build an account aggregator app." The real work was resolving three tensions that make people abandon the flow.

Discovery vs. effort

Account auto-discovery. Adding each bank account by hand is time-consuming, cumbersome and error-prone. Users need accounts identified and integrated automatically.

Consent vs. trust

User consent. People hesitate to let an aggregator access their data when security and privacy are not explained. They need a clear process that builds confidence and protects data.

Control vs. complexity

Account management. Linking, delinking and relinking are complex and confusing. Users need a simple system to manage connected accounts with ease.

North Star: a person can always answer "who can see what, and how do I stop it?" in under 10 seconds.

Decisions and trade-offs

Four primary destinations, built around consent

Decision
Bottom navigation of Home, Accounts, Consents, Profile. Consents is a top-level tab, not a settings sub-page.
Rejected
A single Accounts hub with consents nested under each bank.
Why
Accounts and consents have different lifecycles and different owners (the user vs. the requesting institution). Separating them gives each a clear mental model.

Auto-discovery first, manual search as fallback

Decision
Bank, then asset class, then OTP, then discovered accounts to confirm. "Search manually" appears only when discovery misses.
Rejected
Manual entry of account numbers as the default path.
Why
Removes the most error-prone step. The trade-off is a longer chain of screens, so each step carries one decision and one clear label.

PIN on every state change

Decision
Approve, pause, resume, revoke and delink all require PIN verification.
Rejected
Confirmation dialogs only, or PIN on revoke only.
Why
Protects against accidental or unauthorised taps, and makes security tangible. The cost is friction, so the PIN pad is the same component everywhere.

A timeline for every consent and account

Decision
Plain-language event log: approved, generated, linked, paused, with timestamps and handles.
Why
Turns a regulatory audit requirement into a trust feature. People can verify what happened without contacting support.

Consent as a state machine

The hardest design problem was making four consent states and their legal transitions predictable. I mapped every transition first, then designed each screen to show only the actions valid for the current state.

Timeline

    The solution in detail

    1. Home dashboard

      Net worth, last-refreshed time, and two counters (pending consents, linked accounts) answer "what needs me?" at a glance. New requests can be accepted inline.

    2. Account linking

      Popular banks first, full searchable list second, asset classes (banks, mutual funds, insurance, deposits and more) as a separate step. Empty states lead directly to the next action.

    3. Consent cards

      Frequency, expiry and last refresh are visible on the card. Pause, Revoke and Timeline sit in a consistent row, so users learn one pattern.

    4. Self consent

      Default and Custom consent tabs show request date, duration, frequency, past transaction range, data filter and data life, then a selectable list of accounts before "Approve & Continue".

    5. Account details

      Masked account number, balance, last refresh, Delink and Timeline actions, with expandable account details and transaction history showing debits and credits with timestamps.

    6. Identity and profile

      A VUA ID handle on the home card, primary and secondary mobile numbers, email, language, and pause or delete profile.

    7. Edge cases

      Balance not yet authorised (inline "Accept Consent"), delinked accounts with relink, connection lost, 404, and scheduled-maintenance screens with a clear return time.

    8. Trust at onboarding

      Four screens, skippable, each answering one fear: what it is, who regulates it, how data is protected, and what the user controls.

    9. Design system

      Roboto with four text styles, one primary (#003B79) and one accent (#FF7D00), documented component specs (56px CTAs, 14px radius, 342px card width), custom fintech icons, bank logo tiles, and status colours for success, error and action required.

    The four screens that carry the product: see what needs you, decide with full context, read the record, confirm with a PIN.

    Product success

    CRIF Connect is live as an RBI-licensed Account Aggregator with a consumer app and web login, inside the consent-based data-sharing ecosystem the RBI framework created.

    What I'm proudest of

    • The consent state machine: four states, every transition gated by one PIN pattern, so a regulated process feels predictable.

    • Graceful failure: connection-lost, 404 and scheduled-maintenance screens that keep a financial product calm under stress.
